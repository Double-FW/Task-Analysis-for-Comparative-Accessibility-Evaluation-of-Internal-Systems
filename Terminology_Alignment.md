# Terminology alignment

The inspected [Double-FW Terminology and Principles](https://github.com/Double-FW/Terminology-and-Principles) revision is `219b5feddd271bcdf70ea0ac0c4bc5c62fe9a0f4`. Its [governance](https://github.com/Double-FW/Terminology-and-Principles/blob/219b5feddd271bcdf70ea0ac0c4bc5c62fe9a0f4/GOVERNANCE.md) and [decision register](https://github.com/Double-FW/Terminology-and-Principles/blob/219b5feddd271bcdf70ea0ac0c4bc5c62fe9a0f4/review/DECISIONS.md) distinguish scoped approved resolutions from proposed entry wording. This alignment does not approve every glossary definition or adopt a client policy.

| Distinction | Task-analysis application |
| --- | --- |
| Accessibility and inclusion | Examine barriers and meaningful participation, not only file/component availability or a scan result. |
| Task completion | Record correct intended outcomes, partial/assisted completion, dependencies and consequences; a click or submission alone is insufficient. |
| Task time | State start/end, units, outcome quality, reference, conditions, assistance and uncertainty. Longer time does not by itself prove exclusion or its cause. |
| Content consumption and engagement | Investigate access to meaning and chosen participation; longer engagement may reflect difficulty. |
| User choice and preferences | Verify real available routes and support without inferring diagnosis or a preference from tool use. |
| Equivalent/comparable experience (D02) | State the comparison across outcomes, effort, privacy, availability, control and dignity. Identical interfaces and absolute timing are not the goal. |
| Satisfactory and satisfaction (D01) | Use Satisfactory for the proposed experience principle; satisfaction is a person’s assessment. A positive rating cannot waive a barrier. |
| Emotional lenses (D03) | Feeling Seen, Feeling Capable, Feeling Confident, Feeling Safe and Feeling Valued are intended qualities and research lenses, not feelings assigned from a technical pass. |

Use adopted Double-FW meanings for its methodologies, preserving externally owned meanings and applicability when citing law, standards or contracts (D08). Comparative performance is not WCAG conformance. WCAG robustness, Agentic Robustness and a particular system’s reliability have separate scopes; Feeling Safe does not prove security (D06).

Use named Cognitive UX identifiers `CP-01`–`CP-10` for cross-repository cognitive references (D04). W3C ACT refers to the actual W3C work and rule status. Local ACT-style procedural tests and user-oriented task scripts have their own scopes (D05). Keep HTA node IDs, QA canonical outcomes, local procedures, executions and external criteria distinct, with their owning sources.

Keep observations, participant accounts, measurements, estimates, hypotheses, synthetic examples, proposals and approvals distinct (D07). `unknown`, `not_tested` and `not_applicable` differ; unresolved is an issue/decision state. Technical results, task outcomes and employment-impact conclusions are not interchangeable. The [methodology](TA%20Methodology.md#10-comparative-experience-scoring-model) defines the local record meanings; preserve a linked source’s vocabulary rather than silently replacing it.

Respect identity and language preferences, including Deaf and neurodivergent identities. Interaction contexts overlap and are not diagnoses or mutually exclusive population groups. Do not rank people, infer incapacity or erase participant reports because observation is missing.

Repository publication, a completed template, matching versions or a new export date does not establish organisational approval, study execution or scientific validation. Keep editorial updates, retrieval, testing, source review and human-decision dates separate.
